01 / Stochastic model

CONFIGURABLE EXPERIMENT

Each attacker faces the same n systems independently. In the Bernoulli model, a successful penetration scores 1 and failure scores 0. In the Rademacher model, each step scores +1 or −1 with equal probability, representing a symmetric gain/loss process. The models are illustrative: real attackers and systems need not be independent or identically distributed.

Total Sₙ = Σ XᵢE[Sₙ] = nμ; Var(Sₙ) = nσ²
Average Sₙ/nE = μ; Var = σ²/n
Normalised Sₙ/√nE = √n μ; Var = σ²

Bernoulli: μ = p, σ² = p(1 − p). Rademacher: μ = 0, σ² = 1. A standard normal CLT for raw Sₙ/√n applies directly to Rademacher; Bernoulli must be centred and divided by σ as well.

03 / Variance under three normalisations

THEORY VS SIMULATION

Independence makes the variance of a sum additive. Dividing by n multiplies the variance by 1/n², while dividing by √n multiplies it by 1/n. Consequently, total-score variance increases linearly, average-score variance decreases as 1/n and normalised-score variance stays constant.

Var(Sₙ) = nσ²   ·   Var(Sₙ/n) = σ²/n   ·   Var(Sₙ/√n) = σ²

04 / Law of large numbers (LLN)

CONVERGENCE IN PROBABILITY

For independent identically distributed increments with finite variance, the sample average tends toward μ. Observe the fraction of attackers whose average is at least ε away from μ, and compare it with Chebyshev's upper bound. Increasing n reduces the spread of Sₙ/n, but individual trajectories can fluctuate.

P(|Sₙ/n − μ| ≥ ε) ≤ min(1, σ²/(nε²)) → 0
Fraction of simulated attackers outside εChebyshev upper bound

The bound can be conservative, and the Monte Carlo estimate need not decrease at every consecutive n. If it reaches zero in a finite simulation, that does not prove the true probability is zero.

05 / Central limit theorem (CLT)

NORMAL APPROXIMATION

For Rademacher increments the uncentred score Sₙ/√n already has mean 0 and variance 1. For Bernoulli increments it does not: we use Zₙ = (Sₙ − nμ)/(σ√n) to compare the same standardized statistic with the standard normal density. In both cases the CLT predicts convergence in distribution, not that every observed Zₙ approaches zero.

Zₙ = (Sₙ − nμ)/(σ√n)   ⇒   N(0, 1)

06 / Interpreting n and m

ATTACKER / SYSTEM CONTEXT

n is the duration of each independent attacker's path. A higher n raises the expected Bernoulli total np, but also makes the observed success rate more stable around p. m is the number of independent simulated attackers. Raising m makes the empirical distribution and estimated probabilities more precise; it does not change a single attacker's theoretical distribution.

More systems (higher n)
Typical total fluctuations grow as √n; the variance of the per-system mean contracts as 1/n. For Rademacher, the total has no drift.
More attackers (higher m)
Monte Carlo uncertainty typically decreases approximately as 1/√m. The expected aggregate total across attackers is mnμ and its variance is mnσ² under independence.

07 / Conclusions

Total vs average

The total score Sₙ is not stable: variance increases with n. The average Sₙ/n becomes more predictable, concentrating around the single-step expected score μ. This is the weak law of large numbers, made quantitative here by the variance and Chebyshev inequality.

CLT vs LLN

LLN describes the location of the average, while CLT describes the shape of the remaining random fluctuations. In the Rademacher case Sₙ/√n tends to N(0,1); when μ is nonzero the correct standardization must subtract nμ and divide by σ√n.

Limitations

Independence, fixed p, and equal skill across attackers are modelling assumptions. Real cyber incidents may exhibit correlated systems, changing success probabilities and attacker learning; therefore, this experiment does not forecast operational attack success.

What the simulation establishes

Repeated seeded trials illustrate the moments and limit theorems. Sample statistics vary around their theoretical values; their agreement is not enforced or hard-coded. Larger m improves precision but does not turn a simulation into a mathematical proof.
